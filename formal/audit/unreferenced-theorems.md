# Unreferenced theorems in the Lean models

Generated by `python3 -I scripts/formal/audit-theorems.py --out formal/audit/unreferenced-theorems.md` from the models as built. A report for deciding deletions: nothing has been deleted.

A theorem counts as referenced when another declaration's proof or definition uses it (read from Lean's environment, through the constants Lean generates), an `example` names it, a docs page's `lean:` anchor cites it, a claims ledger under `formal/claims/` names it with its model file, a comment in `src/` or `tests/` names it (as `Model.name`, or in backticks near a mention of its model file), or `Audit.lean` prints its axioms. The rest are listed below, each with a class:

* **(a)** a standalone property worth keeping, ideally cited from a test, doc or ledger;
* **(b)** a leftover or superseded helper, a deletion candidate;
* **(c)** unclear.

The class comes from what the sources say about the theorem (named as a result in its file's header, `formal/README.md` or the CHANGELOG; a bold-titled doc comment, or one that quotes the Rust or names a Rust test; a closed concrete case; a short undocumented lemma; a history-layer stepping stone; a counterpart that supersedes it) and is a first sort for the owner to confirm. `Drain/History.lean` describes no current Rust and is kept as the evidence for its findings; its (b) entries are listed for completeness.
